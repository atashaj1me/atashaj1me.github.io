The Political Economy of China's Contemporary Banking Sector Coursework for Chinese Political Economy, Fulbright University Vietnam · Spring 2026 · Coauthor(s): LE Thien Ha Ngan

Done — last updated 2026-05-20.

The Chinese Communist Party (CCP) perceives finance, dominated by banking activities, as one of its strategic bases of political power. In this framework of Chinese party-state capitalism, the party-state encroaches its influence over China's banking sector by utilizing institutionalized controlling channels such as regulatory control, ownership control, and personnel control. These institutional instruments let the party-state hold absolute authority over banking operations, but also create structural conflicts. We note that these frictions are not systemic failures but an equilibrium outcome regarding power dynamics between the state versus market, intra-state, and intra-market that reflect on-going political relations given institutional features of an authoritarian regime.

Taiwan-China-US Relations and the Geopolitics of the Taiwan Strait Coursework for Geopolitics, Fulbright University Vietnam · Spring 2026 · Coauthor(s): PHAN Minh Khoa, LE Khanh Huyen, VU Linh Nhu

Done — last updated 2026-05-20.

The Taiwan Strait is one of the flashpoint of contemporary global politics, regarding regional stability and semiconductor supply chain. Here, any form of escalation can pose significant geopolitical risks to the global economy. We find that Taiwan is positioning itself as an hedging partner with the US, for security and technological alignment, toward China with balancing mechanism via cross-Strait economic integration and cultural affairs.

Vietnam' Risk of a Middle Income Trap: The Role of Child-Rearing Costs Coursework for Macroeconomic Analysis, Fulbright University Vietnam · Spring 2026

Done — last updated 2026-05-20.

For the last three decades, Vietnam has endorsed the export-led model by attracting foreign direct investment (FDI) via low taxes and cheap labor costs, as well as proximity to China and other trading networks. However, as the country population is aging and the "dividend" of the golden share of labor force is diminishing, Vietnam has to find a new model of growth; otherwise, the middle income trap will be inevitable to escape. To address this risk, I used an overlapping generations model of a closed economy to theorize "fates" of Vietnam. (Note: I can use other and much more fancy models, but the instructor for the course (Dr. Xavier Bautista) demands me to closely stick to the course materials).
