Land-use Right and the Macroeconomy: A DSGE Approach for Vietnam and China Working paper · 2026

In progress — last updated 2026-04.

This paper focus on how land-use right, not land, as land is nominally state-owned in Viet Nam and China, play the key role in the macroeconomy as a source of goverment revenues and asset class in the real estate markets. This is a DSGE model inspired by Cochrane (2023)'s "Fiscal Theory of the Price Level," where I treat "land," sovereign bonds, and money supply (via seigniorage) as the goverment's liabilities.

The Political Economy of China's Contemporary Banking Sector Coursework for Chinese Political Economy, Fulbright University Vietnam · Spring 2026 · Coauthor(s): LE Thien Ha Ngan

In progress — last updated 2026-05.

Finance and politics in China show a significant overlap, reflecting on a maze of institutional arrangements. With China's current model of Chinese Party-State Capitalism, finance is considered as a strategic sector that must be under the control of the party-state, this research aims to analyze the institutional arrangements that underline these influences. We identifis three channels of control—regulatory controls, ownership controls, and personnel controls—which are constructed and maintained via institutionalization processes.

Taiwan-China-US Relations and the Geopolitics of the Taiwan Strait Coursework for Geopolitics, Fulbright University Vietnam · Spring 2026 · Coauthor(s): PHAN Minh Khoa, LE Khanh Huyen, VU Linh Nhu

In progress — last updated 2026-05.

The Taiwan Strait is one of the flashpoint of contemporary global politics, regarding regional stability and semiconductor supply chain. Here, any form of escalation can pose significant geopolitical risks to the global economy. We find that Taiwan is positioning itself as an hedging partner with the US, for security and technological alignment, toward China with balancing mechanism via cross-Strait economic integration and cultural affairs.

Vietnam's Looming Risk of a Middle Income Trap: An Overlapping Generations Model Coursework for Macroeconomic Analysis, Fulbright University Vietnam · Spring 2026

In progress — last updated 2026-05.

For the last three decades, Vietnam has endorsed the export-led model by attracting foreign direct investment (FDI) via low taxes and cheap labor cost, as well as proximity to China and other trading network. However, as the country population is aging and the "dividend" of the golden share of labor force is diminishing, Vietnam has to find a new model of growth elsewhile the middle income trap will be inevitable to escape. To address this risk, I used an overlapping generation model of a closed economy to theorize "fates" of Vietnam. (Note: I can use other and much more fancy models, but the instructor for the course (Dr. Xavier Bautista) demands me to closely stick to the course material).
